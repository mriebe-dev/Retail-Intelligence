Uniqlo continues expansion in France, announcing new store in Cannes

Even for a company already firmly established in France, continued growth still hinges on a finely calibrated regional footprint. For Uniqlo, expansion in France remains a key growth lever, alongside an equally robust rollout across Europe. The Japanese brand has announced the opening of a new store in Cannes in autumn 2026, confirming its strategy of establishing a foothold in high-appeal locations.




The future Uniqlo Cannes store at 68 rue d'Antibes - DR


This opening will take the brand’s total number of stores in France to 31, including three in the Alpes-Maritimes. This growth forms part of a trajectory underway for several years, as the group strengthens its presence both in major cities and in premium destinations.

The brand continues to build out its footprint on the Côte d'Azur, following an initial opening in Cagnes-sur-Mer in 2015 and a more recent launch in Nice in 2024. This latest step strengthens its presence in the region: in the heart of Cannes, at 68 rue d'Antibes, Uniqlo will open a 610-square-metre store that includes an indoor garden—a format aligned with the progressive move upmarket across its retail estate.

This choice of location reflects a clear rationale. Beyond its international profile shaped by the Cannes Film Festival, Cannes enjoys steady tourist flows and a high-spending clientele—an environment perfectly suited to Uniqlo's strategy, which favours high-visibility locations to deploy its LifeWear concept.

"This new location illustrates our desire to offer the LifeWear experience in attractive destinations, in tune with our customers' lifestyles," said Yuki Yamada, managing director, Uniqlo France.

Beyond its role as a showcase, this opening forms part of a broader strategy. In France, as across Europe, the Fast Retailing group’s brand is accelerating its expansion with large-format stores designed to spotlight its full range. The future Cannes store will offer both men's and women's collections, in an environment conceived to fully embody the brand's fundamentals.

This positioning rests on the LifeWear philosophy, a pillar of Uniqlo's identity. Eschewing more ephemeral fashion, the Japanese brand focuses on durable, technical, and accessible basics—an approach that has underpinned its status as a global player, with over 2,500 stores worldwide.




FashionNetwork.com archive of the LifeWear range - DR


In France, this strategy translates into a progressively densified footprint combining major metropolitan areas and highly attractive tourist destinations. Having consolidated its presence in major cities, Uniqlo now sees the Côte d'Azur as a logical area for development, at the crossroads of a discerning local clientele and a constant flow of international visitors.

This move forms part of a broader momentum already at work in the French market. As noted at the start of the year, Uniqlo continues to densify its network, with several projects in progress. This strategy extends beyond the French market, with around fifteen openings planned in Europe for the 2026 financial year.

With this in mind, the Cannes opening also carries an operational dimension. The store is expected to employ 45 people, illustrating the group's determination to support its expansion by strengthening its local teams.

More broadly, this step-up aligns with the trajectory of the Fast Retailing group, which generated almost $23.16 billion in revenue in its 2025 financial year. Alongside brands such as Comptoir des Cotonniers and Theory, Uniqlo remains the group's primary growth driver, fuelled both by an international expansion strategy and a clearly differentiated product positioning. This momentum is particularly visible in Europe, where the region now accounts for more than 13% of the brand's sales, compared with 11% a year earlier, confirming the acceleration of the European market in the group's trajectory.

The task now is to strike the right balance between rapid growth and brand coherence. As the network expands, particularly in markets with high tourist exposure such as the Côte d'Azur, the challenge for the Japanese brand will be to maintain the clarity of its offer and the quality of its customer experience, while adapting to very different trading environments.
