SKIMS, Kim Kardashian’s powerhouse shapewear and apparel brand, has appointed retail veteran Judy Genovese as its new Senior Director of Retail EMEA ahead of its highly anticipated UK brick-and-mortar debut. Bringing premium leadership experience from her previous executive roles at Victoria’s Secret and Ralph Lauren, Genovese will work alongside EMEA President Robin Gendron to build the brand's European physical retail infrastructure from the ground up. In her new role, she will be directly responsible for steering in-store performance, talent development, and strategic new market rollouts across the entire EMEA territory. The high-profile appointment coincides with the unveiling of innovative, sleekly molded storefront hoardings on London's iconic Regent Street, teasing the brand's upcoming 12,000-square-foot flagship store set to open this summer. Securing a 10-year lease with The Crown Estate, the massive space is designed to deliver a fully immersive consumer experience showcasing Skims' signature underwear, loungewear, and shapewear collections.

There’s something interesting happening in retail.

A lingerie brand opening a café might sound random. But it’s actually part of a much bigger shift.

Etam Café is just one example.

You see it with Ralph Lauren and its cafés,
with Gucci opening Gucci Osteria,
or even Armani building Armani Caffè.

Different brands, same idea: turn retail into a place people choose to spend time in.

And it’s not just fashion.

Nike creates stores where you can train, customize and interact. 
Apple turned stores into spaces to explore, learn and hang out.
Starbucks built an entire business on being a “third place” — not home, not work.

So what’s the common thread?

These brands are not just selling products.
They’re designing environments.

Places where you stay longer, feel something, and naturally build a connection.

Because the strategy isn’t the café.
Or the event.
Or the space.

The strategy is time + emotion

The longer you stay,
the more you engage,
the more likely you are to come back.

And that’s where retail is heading:
less transactional, more experiential.

💥Fashion’s biggest winners 2025 are not necessarily the brands with the most hype. 

They’re the companies with the strongest systems. 📊

The latest global apparel market cap rankings reveal something very important about where investors see long-term value being created.

At the top of the hierarchy we find:

* LVMH — approx. $276B LVMH  
* Hermès — approx. $205B Hermès  
* Inditex — approx. $195B Inditex  
* The TJX Companies — approx. $175B TK MAXX  
* Fast Retailing — approx. $133B Fast Retailing  

And then another powerful layer including:

* Christian Dior SE Christian Dior Couture  
* Cintas 
* Nike Nike  
* Ross Stores
* Kering Kering  

What’s fascinating is that these companies operate with completely different business models:

Luxury.
Fast fashion.
Off-price retail.
Uniform services.
Performance sportswear.

Yet capital markets are rewarding the same core capabilities across all of them:

-Inventory control
-Operational efficiency
-Supply chain execution
-Pricing power
-Global scalability
-Strong cash generation

One of the clearest signals in this ranking is the continued rise of the off-price model.

The The TJX Companies, Inc.  Companies and ROSS DRESS FOR LESS  are no longer viewed as “discount retailers” in the old sense of the word.

They are now seen as highly sophisticated inventory redistribution platforms capable of monetizing market inefficiencies faster than many traditional full-price retailers.

At the same time, companies like Inditex continue proving that speed, vertical integration, and rapid inventory rotation remain massive competitive advantages in modern retail.

And luxury groups such as LVMH and Hermès keep demonstrating that scarcity, brand control, and disciplined distribution still command extraordinary investor confidence.

The message from the market seems very clear:

In today’s apparel industry, valuation is no longer driven only by creativity or branding.

It is increasingly driven by operational intelligence. 🚀
